# Animal Farm — George Orwell

## Plot

### Old Major's dream

The story begins one evening, when Old Major, an old pig respected by all, gathers the animals in the barn to tell them a dream. He feels he will die soon and wants to leave a message: the life of animals is sad and hard — they work for men and end up killed. The real enemy is man, the only creature who consumes without producing. Without men, animals would be happy. He tells them to rebel and teaches them *Beasts of England*, the song of the revolution.

Three days later Old Major dies, but his ideas remain. Three young pigs — Snowball, Napoleon and Squealer — develop Animalism, a system of thought.

### The Rebellion

One evening Mr. Jones comes home drunk and forgets to feed the animals. Hungry, they break into the food store. When Jones and his men arrive with whips, the animals attack them and chase them out of the farm. The revolution happens almost by accident, with no plan.

They rename the farm "Animal Farm". The pigs, who learned to read and write during the winter, write the Seven Commandments on the wall of the big barn. These are the rules of the new society:

1. Whatever goes on two legs is an enemy.
2. Whatever goes on four legs, or has wings, is a friend.
3. No animal shall wear clothes.
4. No animal shall sleep in a bed.
5. No animal shall drink alcohol.
6. No animal shall kill any other animal.
7. All animals are equal.

### The first days of the free farm

At first everything goes well: the animals work with joy and harvest the hay better than the men. Boxer, the big cart-horse, is the strongest and most loyal, and repeats two mottos: "I will work harder" and "Napoleon is always right". Snowball forms groups for the work and tries to teach everyone to read, but most cannot: the sheep only repeat "Four legs good, two legs bad".

Already the pigs start to have privileges. The milk and the fallen apples are kept only for them. Squealer, the pig who can convince anyone, explains they need it to think well and run the farm — nobody protests.

News of the rebellion reaches other farms, and Mr. Jones returns with armed neighbours. They win the Battle of the Cowshed thanks to the courage of Snowball, who is wounded and receives a medal. Boxer also fights bravely and thinks he has killed a boy (the boy was only knocked out). He is very upset by the idea of taking a life.

### The fight between Snowball and Napoleon

Between the two leaders the competition grows. Snowball speaks well, full of ideas to improve the farm. Napoleon speaks little but works behind the scenes for his own power.

The key moment comes when Snowball wants to build a windmill, to bring electricity and to make the work less hard. Napoleon is against it. At the assembly, when Snowball is about to win, Napoleon whistles: nine huge fierce dogs enter the barn — he had raised them in secret since puppies as his personal guard. The dogs attack Snowball, who escapes by jumping over a hedge. Snowball is never seen again.

Napoleon takes full power and abolishes the assemblies: a group of pigs led by him takes all decisions. Days later he announces the windmill will be built, claiming the idea was his, stolen by Snowball.

### Napoleon's dictatorship

From now on the farm changes. Animals work harder and harder for the windmill, pigs less and less. Napoleon trades with the men of neighbouring farms, which Old Major had forbidden. The pigs move into the farmhouse and sleep in the beds. When some notice this breaks the fourth commandment, they read the wall: it now says "No animal shall sleep in a bed with sheets". Nobody remembered that detail. The same happens to all other commandments, changed in secret one after the other.

One night a storm destroys the almost finished windmill. Napoleon blames Snowball for sabotage, and he becomes the cause of everything that goes wrong. The animals must rebuild from the start, with smaller food rations.

Things get worse. When the hens refuse to give the eggs Napoleon wants to sell, he starves them, and some die. Then he organises a public trial: terrified by the dogs, some animals confess to crimes they never did (working with Snowball, planning against Napoleon) and are killed in front of everyone. It is the first blood spilled on the farm. Boxer does not understand and only repeats: "It must be our fault. I will work harder". The sixth commandment now says: "No animal shall kill any other animal without reason".

### The second battle and the end of Boxer

The windmill is rebuilt. Napoleon sells wood to Frederick, owner of a neighbouring farm, who pays with fake money and then attacks Animal Farm with guns. In the Battle of the Windmill the animals win, but the windmill is blown up with dynamite and many die. Squealer still presents the battle as a great victory. The same evening the pigs find a case of whisky in the farmhouse and get drunk: the next day the fifth commandment says "No animal shall drink alcohol to excess".

Boxer keeps working too hard to rebuild the windmill, though old and ill. One day he collapses while pulling a stone. Squealer announces Napoleon will send him to the hospital in town to be cured. But when the van arrives, the donkey Benjamin — the only one who reads well — reads the side: "Horse Slaughterer. Glue and Bone Supplier". Boxer is sold to the horse butcher. The others try to warn him, but he is too weak to kick the door. A few days later, Squealer says that Boxer died in hospital with the best care, and that his last words were "Forward, in the name of Napoleon". Right after, the pigs receive a new case of whisky bought with the sale money.

### All animals are equal, but some are more equal

Years pass. Few animals remember how life was before the Rebellion. The farm has grown, with a new windmill, but for the animals nothing has changed since Mr. Jones: they work a lot, eat little, are cold in winter. Only the pigs and the dogs live well.

One day they see an unbelievable scene: Squealer is walking on two legs. A moment later all the pigs come out standing up, and Napoleon last with a whip in his hand. The sheep, trained by Squealer, bleat a new slogan: "Four legs good, two legs better". On the wall only one sentence is left:

> All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.

That evening the pigs invite the owners of the neighbouring farms into the farmhouse — the men they used to fight. The other animals, curious, come close to the window. They see pigs and men sitting together at the table, playing cards, drinking and toasting. Mr. Pilkington gives a speech: he fully agrees with Napoleon, because he too has lower classes to control. Napoleon answers that the farm will go back to its old name, Manor Farm.

Then a fight breaks out between Napoleon and Pilkington, because both cheated at cards. Here is the final sentence of the novel, one of the most famous in 20th century literature: the animals outside look from pig to man and from man to pig, and again from pig to man, but they can no longer tell who is who.
