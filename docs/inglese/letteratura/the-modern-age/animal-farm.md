# Animal Farm — George Orwell

*Animal Farm* is a short novel by George Orwell, published in 1945. Orwell called it "a fairy tale", because the main characters are talking animals, but it is really a political satire of the Russian Revolution: it shows how a revolution born to free the people ended in Stalin's dictatorship. The book has ten chapters and takes place at Manor Farm, an English farm owned by Mr. Jones, a cruel and drunken man.

## Plot

### Old Major's dream

The story begins one evening, when Old Major, an old pig respected by all the animals, calls them together in the barn to tell them a dream. He feels he is going to die soon, and he wants to leave a message. He explains that the life of animals is sad and hard: they work for men, and in the end they are killed. The real enemy is man, the only creature who consumes without producing. Without men, the animals would be happy. He tells them to rebel, and he teaches them a song, *Beasts of England*, which will become the song of the revolution.

Three days later Old Major dies, but his ideas stay. Three young pigs — Snowball, Napoleon and Squealer — take his teachings and turn them into a system of thought called Animalism.

### The Rebellion

One evening Mr. Jones comes home drunk and forgets to feed the animals. They are hungry and break into the food store. When Jones and his men arrive with whips, the animals attack them and chase them out of the farm. The revolution happens almost by accident, with no plan.

The animals take the farm and rename it "Animal Farm". The pigs, who have learned to read and write during the winter, write the Seven Commandments on the wall of the big barn. These are the rules of the new society:

1. Whatever goes on two legs is an enemy.
2. Whatever goes on four legs, or has wings, is a friend.
3. No animal shall wear clothes.
4. No animal shall sleep in a bed.
5. No animal shall drink alcohol.
6. No animal shall kill any other animal.
7. All animals are equal.

### The first days of the free farm

At the beginning, everything seems to go well. The animals work with joy and bring in the hay better than the men did. Boxer, the big cart-horse, is the strongest and the most loyal worker. He always repeats two mottos: "I will work harder" and "Napoleon is always right". Snowball makes groups to organise the work and tries to teach all the animals to read, but most of them cannot learn. The sheep only repeat the slogan "Four legs good, two legs bad".

But already now the pigs start to have privileges. The milk and the apples that fall from the trees are kept only for them. Squealer, the pig who can convince anyone, explains that the pigs need this food to think well and to run the farm. Nobody protests.

The news of the rebellion reaches other farms, and Mr. Jones comes back with armed neighbours. The animals win the Battle of the Cowshed thanks to the courage of Snowball, who is wounded and receives a medal. Boxer also fights bravely and thinks he has killed a boy (the boy was only knocked out). He is very upset by the idea of taking a life.

### The fight between Snowball and Napoleon

Between the two pig leaders, the competition becomes stronger and stronger. Snowball is a good speaker, full of ideas to make the farm better. Napoleon speaks little, but he works behind the scenes to build his own power.

The key moment comes when Snowball wants to build a windmill, to bring electricity and to make the work less hard. Napoleon is against it. During the assembly, when Snowball is about to win, Napoleon makes a strange whistle. Nine huge fierce dogs enter the barn: Napoleon took them as puppies and raised them in secret as his personal guard. The dogs attack Snowball, who escapes just in time by jumping over a hedge. From that moment, Snowball is never seen again.

Napoleon takes full power. He abolishes the assemblies: from now on, a group of pigs, led by him, will take all decisions. A few days later he also says the windmill will be built — and he says it was his own idea, stolen by Snowball.

### Napoleon's dictatorship

From this moment, the farm changes. The animals work harder and harder for the windmill, while the pigs work less and less. Napoleon starts to trade with the men of the neighbouring farms, which Old Major had forbidden. The pigs move into the farmhouse and sleep in the beds. When some animals notice that this breaks the fourth commandment, they go to read the wall and find that the commandment now says "No animal shall sleep in a bed with sheets". Nobody remembered that detail. The same thing happens with all the other commandments: they are changed in secret, one after the other.

One night a storm destroys the windmill, which was almost finished. Napoleon blames Snowball for the sabotage. From now on, Snowball is the cause of everything that goes wrong on the farm. The animals must build the windmill again from the start, with smaller food rations.

Things get worse. When the hens refuse to give the eggs that Napoleon wants to sell to the humans, he starves them, and some die. Then he organises a kind of public trial: some animals, terrified by the dogs, confess to crimes they never did (working with Snowball, planning against Napoleon) and are killed in front of everyone. It is the first blood spilled inside the farm. Boxer does not understand, and only repeats: "It must be our fault. I will work harder". The sixth commandment now says: "No animal shall kill any other animal without reason".

### The second battle and the end of Boxer

The windmill is rebuilt. Napoleon sells a pile of wood to Frederick, the owner of a neighbouring farm, who pays with fake money and right after attacks Animal Farm with guns. In the Battle of the Windmill, the animals win, but the windmill is destroyed with dynamite and many die. Squealer still presents the battle as a great victory. The same evening, the pigs find a case of whisky in the farmhouse and get drunk: the next day the fifth commandment says "No animal shall drink alcohol to excess".

Boxer keeps working too hard to rebuild the windmill, even though he is old and ill. One day he falls down while pulling a stone. Squealer announces that Napoleon will send him to the hospital in town to be cured. But when the van arrives, the donkey Benjamin — the only animal who can really read — reads what is written on the side: "Horse Slaughterer. Glue and Bone Supplier". Boxer is sold to the horse butcher. The other animals try to warn him, but he is too weak to kick and break the door. A few days later, Squealer says that Boxer died in hospital with the best care, and that his last words were "Forward, in the name of Napoleon". Right after, the pigs get a new case of whisky bought with the money from the sale.

### All animals are equal, but some are more equal

Years pass. Few animals remember how life was before the Rebellion. The farm has grown, and there is a new windmill, but for the animals nothing has changed since the time of Mr. Jones: they work a lot, they eat little, they are cold in winter. Only the pigs and the dogs live well.

One day the animals see an unbelievable scene: Squealer is walking on two legs. A moment later, all the pigs come out standing up, and Napoleon comes out last with a whip in his hand. The sheep, trained by Squealer, start to bleat a new slogan: "Four legs good, two legs better". On the wall of the Seven Commandments only one sentence is left:

> All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.

That evening, the pigs invite into the farmhouse the owners of the neighbouring farms — the men with whom they used to be at war. The other animals, curious, come close to the window. They see pigs and men sitting together at the table, playing cards, drinking and making toasts. Mr. Pilkington makes a speech and says that he fully agrees with Napoleon, because he too has lower classes to control. Napoleon answers by saying that the farm will go back to its old name, Manor Farm.

Then a fight breaks out between Napoleon and Pilkington, because both of them cheated at cards. Here is the final sentence of the novel, one of the most famous in 20th century literature: the animals outside look from pig to man and from man to pig, and again from pig to man, but they can no longer tell who is who.

## Why it ends like this

The plot shows how a revolution made in the name of equality can turn into its opposite. The key point is not a single event, but the way the changes happen little by little: a commandment changed, a new privilege for the pigs, a truth rewritten by Squealer. When the animals notice, it is too late: the new masters are now the same as the old ones.
